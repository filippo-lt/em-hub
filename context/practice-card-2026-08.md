# Practice Card — four weeks, three drills

**Started:** 24 August 2026
**Goal:** craft, not position. Close the gap between reading a situation correctly and converting that read into a decision, a date, a claim, or a consequence.
**Review:** 21 September 2026 — before the reorg lands and before Victor arrives.

---

## The diagnosis in one line

**You close on other people's behalf and not on your own.** With Vlad and Andrey you name owners, dates and gate conditions consistently. With David — and about yourself — you don't. You have the skill. You just never point it at anything where you are the subject.

## The belief underneath it

**You think your job is to absorb.** Every blind spot is rational if that's true: don't price scope (it pushes cost outward), don't claim results (that's a claim on someone's attention), ration asks (asking is imposing), surface problems not wins (problems are useful, wins are self-serving), decline help (that's what absorbing means).

It's what made you good at the IC-to-EM transition. It's now the ceiling.

## Two reframes that make the drills possible

**1. Narration is a data-quality problem, not a vanity problem.**
When you don't say "TraceCheck launched clean — Stripe, billing, legal, paywall", your CTO's investment decisions run on a bug-shaped picture. When you don't say "that pushes X to Y", someone plans against a date you privately know is wrong. You are not promoting yourself. You are refusing to mis-route information through the organisation that runs on it.

**2. Absorbing is a tax you pass down, not a shield you hold up.**
Because nobody narrated Andrey's launch, Alex's framing became the record — *"he leverages a lot on AI"* — and turned into a job posting describing your engineer's deficiency. David made the counter-argument himself, unprompted, forty minutes later. **The absorber believes he's protecting people. Often he's only delaying the moment they find out.**

## Drill 1 — The price sentence · Weeks 1–4

**Trigger:** anyone senior asks you to take something on.

**Move — one sentence, always the same shape:**

> "Yes — [deliverable] by [date]. That pushes [other thing] to [date]."

If you don't know what it displaces, say that instead: *"Yes. Let me come back tomorrow with a date and what it moves."*

**Why this one is first:** it's the root behaviour caught at the exact moment it happens, and it costs no courage. It's a scheduling statement, not a confrontation. You already do it for Vlad every week.

**Banned this month:** "Sure." · "Sure, whatever." · "Sounds good." · "I'm happy with this." · "All good."
Each of these appeared in the record at a moment where a name, a date or a condition belonged.

**Score weekly:** items accepted from David ___ · of those, with a date said out loud ___
**Target:** 100% by week 3. Baseline 24 Aug: seven accepted, two dated.

## Drill 2 — The Friday note · Weeks 2–4

**Trigger:** Friday 16:00, recurring calendar block. Non-negotiable.

**Move:** ten lines to David. Three headings, nothing else.

```
Shipped   — what landed, and what it means (not what you did — what changed)
Blocked   — what's stuck, who owns it, what I need from you
Deciding  — what I'm about to decide, so you can object before I do
```

**Why:** writing is your strongest medium and the room is your weakest. This moves the load to where you're good. It also cannot lose to whatever is live on Monday — which is the exact mechanism that has killed `martech-guidelines` four times running.

**And he asked for it.** 24 Aug, in his own words: *"please elaborate as much as you can on me in order to have more information."*

**Score weekly:** sent Y/N · items in "Shipped" that you'd previously have left unsaid ___

---

## Drill 3 — The first three minutes · Weeks 3–4

**Trigger:** any meeting where you're not the most senior person.

**Move:** three items, written at the top of the prep, said **before** the other person opens. The structural one first — never the urgent one.

Fixed count is the point. A fixed count can't be eaten by triage, which is what happens every time you carry one item and something live arrives.

**Why third:** this is the one that takes actual nerve. Build the first two, then this. By week 3 the Friday note has already removed most of what used to compete for the slot.

**Score weekly:** items raised before he set the agenda ___ / 3

## The thing to stop

**Stop declining help.** Twice on 4 August David checked whether you were burning out and you deflected both times — on a day you'd told Andrey you were *"very, very tired."* You are absorbing downward and refusing upward, which is the wrong direction for both relationships.

Managing your own capacity is part of the craft, and your reports are watching how you do it. A manager who won't take help teaches his team not to ask for it.

---

## What NOT to change

The fix is **not** "be more assertive." Do not become louder, more political, or more self-promoting in style — that would damage the thing that already works.

What works, and should stay exactly as it is:

- **Structural, non-personal diagnosis.** *"The PO is way better than the PM — you can notice because the PO has been off three weeks and they are completely stopped."* · *"Victor not being part of any apps team is detached from the actual reality of things."* David adopts these on the spot, every time.
- **Intellectual honesty under pressure.** You declined the easy exit on the AI-test failure and took the hit yourself. It raised your standing, not lowered it.
- **Telling the person before you escalate.** You told Victor about the audit findings the same morning and said so in the room. It changed David's posture from defending to solving. Most managers skip this.
- **Claiming the hard conversation.** You took the Deel conversation with Andrey rather than routing it to HR, with a fallback built in, and you pre-tested the boundary before walking in. That is high craft and it's the best-executed thing in the file.

You are not a weak manager with a confidence problem. You are a strong diagnostician with a conversion problem.

## Use the system you already built

EM Hub is a deliberate-practice machine and you've been running it in diagnostic mode only — it detects misses, it doesn't drill fixes. Add a practice layer:

- **Top of every `_prep.md`:** the three drill lines, blank.
- **Bottom of every `_analysis.md`:** rep counts, not just misses. *Accepted/dated. Note sent Y/N. Raised before he opened: n/3.*
- **A running tally** across four weeks, so you can see a trend rather than re-reading a verdict.
