# 1-on-1 with Andrey — 9 March 2026

## Meeting Structure

Two blocks. Start with the comfort zone, then move to the big conversation.

## Block 1 — AI Design: New Team Onboarding

**Context:** New Anadea team arrives tomorrow. They already have access to all projects. This is about getting them productive fast.

**What you need from Andrey:**
- Set up onboarding for the new team: architecture walkthrough, coding standards, PR review process, how the sprint works, who does what.
- He's done this before (Dimitro, Vlad) and insists on doing it himself. Let him own it.

**Non-negotiable: Cursor adoption.**
- Anadea is contractually required to use Cursor as their AI coding tool. This is not optional.
- Andrey needs to drive this with the new team from day one — making sure they're set up with Cursor, using it actively, and following whatever rules/skills we have in place.
- He has opinions about Cursor vs. Codex (he's said Cursor is "last year's technology"). Acknowledge his view, but be direct: "The contract says Cursor. You need to drive adoption with the team. I need you fully on board with this — not just tolerating it, actively championing it."
- If he pushes back, stay firm: "I hear you. The company and the contract have made this decision. Your job is to make it work as well as possible for the team. Channel your expertise into making the Cursor experience better — rules, skills, configurations — rather than fighting the choice."

**Questions to ask:**
- "What's your plan for onboarding them this week?"
- "Are you set up to get them using Cursor from day one?"
- "What do you need from me to make this smooth?"

**Success for Block 1:** Andrey has a concrete onboarding plan and is committed to driving Cursor adoption with the new team. No ambiguity.

## Block 2 — Phase 2 Transition: iMote Rebuild + Shared Library

**Context:** This is the career-defining conversation. Andrey transitions from overseeing contractors to hands-on engineering — rebuilding iMote from scratch with AI tools, while coordinating Anadea to extract a shared library usable by Screen Mirroring.

### How to Open

Lead with what he already knows and believes:
- "You've told me multiple times the iMote codebase is in terrible shape. You're right."
- "AI feature work on it would be counterproductive. The only path is a rebuild."
- "You're the person who knows this app best. You define the architecture, you set the standards, you build it to your specs."

Then lay out the plan clearly:
- **Andrey rebuilds iMote from scratch** against the existing backend API. New iOS app, clean codebase, his architecture.
- **Shared library extraction** — Serhii Kostrykin (Anadea) works on both iMote and Screen Mirroring and knows where the overlap lives. Andrey writes the spec (API contract, module boundaries, what's in/out), Anadea builds to it.
- **Week 1 deliverable:** shared library spec — first draft by end of this week.
- **First coding task:** deliberately small. One Jira ticket, one PR, merged. Prove the workflow before scaling it.

### Expect Concerns — Address Them All Today

He will raise objections. The goal is not to shut them down — it's to sort them into **real blockers** (solve them) and **fear-based resistance** (acknowledge and move past). 

Likely concerns and how to handle each:

| He says... | What's behind it | Your response |
|---|---|---|
| "I'm not sure I can rebuild a whole app" | Identity threat — fear of failing publicly | "You're not building it alone. Anadea handles the shared library. You start with one small feature. We scale from there." |
| "Cursor isn't good enough for this" | Legitimate technical concern + familiar objection | "Use what works for now. If Cursor falls short on specific tasks, document it — I'll take that data upward. But the work starts regardless." |
| "What about my current responsibilities?" | Overwhelm — two projects + oversight + new thing | "AI Design keeps running — the new team handles day-to-day. Your role there shifts to standards and oversight. iMote rebuild becomes your primary focus." |
| "The contractors are still there, why me?" | Doesn't see the strategic picture | "David's core question is whether internal engineers can replace contractors. This is how we prove it. If it works, your position is bulletproof. If we don't prove it, everyone's position is at risk." |
| "I need more time to think about it" | Stalling — wants to avoid committing | "I understand this is big. But the timeline is real. I need your commitment today to start on the spec this week. We can iterate on the details as we go." |
| "What if the rebuild fails?" | Fear of being blamed | "This is a controlled experiment. The old app keeps running with contractors as a safety net. We're not betting the product on day one." |

### The Commitment You Need

Be explicit about what "commitment" means:
1. He agrees the iMote rebuild is his primary workstream starting now.
2. He delivers a first draft of the shared library spec by end of this week.
3. He starts his first small coding task (one ticket, one PR) by early next week.
4. He drives the approach — architecture decisions are his to make.

If he agrees with caveats, that's fine — write them down and solve them. If he says "I'll try," push: "I need more than 'I'll try.' What specifically would make this a 'yes'?"

### Framing That Works For Him

From his profile — he responds to:
- **Logical reasoning:** "The codebase is broken. Feature work on it is waste. Rebuild is the only rational path."
- **Autonomy with guardrails:** "You define the architecture. I handle the org politics. Anadea builds to your spec."
- **Clear ownership:** "This is yours. Not shared, not committee-driven. You."
- **Identity-safe language:** He's the architect and standard-setter, not someone being forced to learn new tricks. Same craft, clean canvas.

### What NOT to Do

- Don't compare him to Vlad. Don't mention Vlad is rebuilding Face AI. If he asks, keep it brief: "Vlad has his own scope. This conversation is about yours."
- Don't dismiss concerns without acknowledging them first. Catalog every one, then triage.
- Don't escalate emotional intensity. Stay calm, structured, logical — that's his language.
- Don't let "less problems, more solutions" come across as dismissive. Frame it as: "I want to hear every concern. For each one, let's figure out the solution together — today."

## Carry-overs (Quick Check Only)

These are secondary today. Touch them briefly if time allows, or defer:
- Dimitro PR quality — trending how?
- Story points in iMote — where are we?
- Vlad induction — did it happen?
- Sergey engagement — any change?
- Risk spreadsheet — maintained?

## Watch For

- **The moment he shifts from objecting to problem-solving.** That's the signal he's in. When you hear "well, what if we..." instead of "I can't because..." — he's committed. Name it: "That's exactly the kind of thinking I need from you."
- **Silence or withdrawal.** If he goes quiet, he's processing or shutting down. Give him space: "Take a second. What's on your mind?"
- **Overwhelm stacking.** New team onboarding + iMote rebuild + shared library + existing oversight. If he's drowning, prioritise for him: "This week: onboarding plan for the new team + shared library spec draft. That's it. Everything else can wait."

## Success Looks Like

Andrey walks out with two clear commitments:
1. **AI Design:** onboarding plan ready, Cursor adoption driven from day one.
2. **iMote rebuild:** committed to start, shared library spec draft by end of week, first small PR early next week.

He sees himself as the architect of the rebuild, not as someone being pushed into something uncomfortable. His concerns have been heard and addressed — not dismissed. The plan is his to shape, the timeline is yours to hold.
