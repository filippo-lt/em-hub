## Key outcomes

### The security exchange — and the sentence you didn't say

You told him about the exposed endpoint. He reacted hard (*"Fuck."*), then recovered and thanked you
**three separate times**. He gave you a date (*"until the 18th"*, then *"at least a plan in September"*),
told you AI Design outranks everything else, and asked for automated LLM scanning across all apps.

**But look at what he said, and what he didn't know:**

> **David:** *"We are lucky. **No one has stolen our keys.**"*

That is a guess, and **a stronger one than the evidence supports.** You checked what could be checked
and found nothing — but log retention only covers a recent window, and nobody yet knows how long
`debug/env` has been live. Saying "clean" would have handed him a claim that a single old billing
anomaly could destroy, with your name on it. **You were right to hold it back.**

But silence leaves his version standing, and his version is the overclaim. The honest formulation is
narrower than either:

> "I checked everything we can check — recent logs and the billing trend — and there's no anomaly.
> Beyond the retention window we have no data, so I can't tell you it was never used. I'm treating
> the keys as burned and rotating all of them."

**That is stronger than "we were lucky", not weaker.** It gives him something true to repeat, and it
protects you both if something surfaces later.

**Two things would actually answer the question, and neither is a log:**

1. **When was `debug/env` deployed?** Git and deploy history give the real exposure window. Vlad
   offered to trace who introduced it and you said *"no need, I know."* You know the who — the
   **when** is the number security will eventually ask for.
2. **Was data-access logging even switched on?** If it wasn't, the recent window is empty too, and
   that changes the sentence from "no anomaly" to "no visibility." Worth confirming before writing.

He is visibly rattled — *"during these last 3 weeks I am kind of paranoid… whoa, whoa, I am alive."*
A bounded, honest answer settles him better than either reassurance or silence.
