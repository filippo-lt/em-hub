## Why 160% is the optimistic reading

**1. Advisory work arrives as interrupts, not as a block.**

```
 On paper:    [ TraceCheck ─────────────────────────── ][ advisory ]

 In reality:  [TC][a][TC][a][ TC ][a][TC][a][ TC ][a][TC][a][TC][a]
               three vendors · three backlogs · three sets of questions
```

The 100% doesn't shrink neatly to 40%. It **fragments** — and TraceCheck is the deep-focus work
of the two. Three advisory relationships cost more than 60% of a person even when they add up
to 60% on a sheet.

**2. TraceCheck isn't a steady-state 1.0.** From 1 Nov he is the *only* engineer on it: two-week
sprints, a roadmap Alex presented to the CEO, no team lead, no PO, and a codebase built with
agents by someone who has left.

**3. He isn't trained yet.** 1–2h/day this week, first small features 30 Sep, "independent"
31 Oct — on roughly 12–20 hours of total exposure, with a holiday somewhere in between whose
dates are still unpinned.

**4. Two of the three apps are not quiet.** AI Design is entering a growth phase with more
marketing spend *and* carrying the security remediation. Face AI carries the same Firestore
work. An advisor on an app in active remediation is not a 20% engagement.
