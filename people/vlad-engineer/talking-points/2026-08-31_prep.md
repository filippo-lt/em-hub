## §1 — AI Design: screen sizes in iOS 27

### What is actually changing

This is not a "new device, new resolution" year. It is a **new aspect ratio and a resizability
mandate**, which is a different class of problem.

```
  Every iPhone AI Design has ever seen      What iOS 27 adds
  ┌──────┐                                  ┌────────┐   ┌────────────────┐
  │      │                                  │        │   │                │
  │      │  ~19.5:9                         │  4:3   │   │      4:3       │
  │      │  one family of ratios            │  5.5"  │   │     7.8"       │
  │      │  portrait-first                  │ outer  │   │     inner      │
  │      │  fixed layouts survive           │        │   │                │
  └──────┘                                  └────────┘   └────────────────┘
                                            2088×1422     2713×1920
                                                 └── and every width in between,
                                                     live, while it unfolds
```

Three facts that matter:

1. **The foldable is iPad-shaped, not iPhone-shaped.** ~5.5" 4:3 outer, ~7.8" 4:3 inner. Reports say
   iOS 27 supports two apps side-by-side on the inner display with iPadOS-style left sidebars.
2. **🔴 Rebuilding against the iOS 27 SDK automatically opts your app into resizability.** You do not
   opt in. You get it by recompiling. Apple's guidance to developers is to stop designing for specific
   devices and fixed orientations and target "a dynamic range of sizes and aspect ratios."
3. **You can test all of this today, on no new hardware.** Apple shipped a resizable iOS simulator,
   Xcode Previews at arbitrary aspect ratios, and coding-agent skills that find common resizability
   breaks. Vlad said on 24 Aug *"I don't really know what's planned to be in iOS 27"* — that is now a
   half-day of work to fix, not a research project.

### Separate the two risks — they have different owners and different dates

| | Risk | Testable | Deadline |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **The build already on the App Store** behaves badly on the new sizes | Today, resizable simulator | Before 9 Sep — the moment shapes are public, business will ask |
| **B** | **The next build**, compiled on the iOS 27 SDK, silently becomes resizable and breaks layouts that were fine | Today, same tooling | Before you cut the autumn release |

**B is the dangerous one, because you get it by doing nothing.** Nobody has to make a decision for it
to happen — someone just bumps the SDK.

### The decision to leave the call with

> **Do we cut the autumn AI Design release on the iOS 26 SDK or the iOS 27 SDK?**

On 26: nothing changes, problem deferred to the next release. On 27: resizability has to be verified
first. That's a decision with a date on it, not a testing task.

### Where it will actually break

Ask him to check these four, in this order — they are ordered by revenue impact, not by difficulty:

1. **Paywall** — the classic. Fixed-height sheets and hardcoded frames. If it breaks, you lose money silently.
2. **Onboarding** — first run, worst possible place to letterbox.
3. **The design canvas / before-after result view** — this app is a *visual* product. Aspect-ratio
   assumptions live here by definition.
4. **Anything with a hardcoded frame, fixed constraint or storyboard-baked size.**

One diagnostic question decides how expensive this is:

> *"Is AI Design SwiftUI with scene lifecycle, or UIKit with fixed constraints?"*

SwiftUI with scene lifecycle is reportedly most of the way there for free. UIKit with storyboards is not.
